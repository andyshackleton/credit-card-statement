Look at the cost as well as the monthly payment

Try 12 and 24 months on the same £2,000 holiday. Compare the monthly payment and total interest for each duration. The final payment can be smaller because the planner stops once the balance reaches zero.

When paying by credit card can make sense

Section 75 can protect eligible purchases costing more than £100 and up to £30,000 if the supplier breaches the contract or misrepresents the purchase. In some cases, paying just the deposit by credit card can qualify. Check MoneyHelper’s guidance on holiday bookings and Section 75.

What to weigh up if you spread the cost

At the standard purchase rate, interest can raise the total and leave you paying for the holiday after you get home. Use the planner to compare the total cost and the number of months each payment would take.

Check your statement before using the estimate

Your statement shows the minimum payment and due date. Use those figures to check any payment you model. If your purchase qualifies for an interest-free period and you pay the required balance in full by the due date, the purchase may cost less than this estimate.
