Paying off credit card debt

Debt avalanche vs debt snowball

The avalanche puts extra money towards the card with the highest interest rate. The snowball puts it towards the card with the smallest balance. Both start by covering every card’s minimum payment. The difference is where the rest goes.

Same cards, same £200 a month

  • Card A has a £1,500 balance, a 2.5% monthly rate and a £50 modelled minimum.
  • Card B has a £500 balance, a 1% monthly rate and a £25 modelled minimum.

After the two minimum payments, there’s £125 left in the first month. The avalanche puts that towards Card A. The snowball puts it towards Card B. Any money left over when a card clears goes to the other card that month.

The avalanche saves £55.68 in this example. The snowball clears a card six months sooner, and both methods finish in month 12. Research suggests that seeing early progress on a smaller balance can motivate some borrowers to keep repaying.

How the estimate works. The example adds interest once a month, rounded to the penny, before applying payments. It assumes fixed rates and minimums, no new spending and no fees. The final payment is capped at the amount owed. Actual statements may use daily interest and changing minimum payments.

What to check before comparing your own cards

Use current balances and the rates that apply to them. A promotional expiry or a card with several rates can change the comparison. If your budget falls short of the combined minimums, you can find free, confidential debt advice through MoneyHelper.
