Paying off credit card debt

How to pay off credit card debt in the UK

Start with the latest statement for each card. Write down the balance, the minimum payment, the due date and the interest rate that applies to the balance. Check whether different parts of the balance are charged at different rates and whether any promotional rate has an end date.

If you have one card, compare fixed monthly payments

Work out what fits after essential bills and priority debts. If you can afford a fixed monthly amount above the minimum, compare it with a higher amount and see how the estimated payoff date and interest change. Check the minimum on every statement. If it exceeds the amount you planned to pay, your plan needs revisiting.

A payoff estimate is easiest to interpret when it assumes no new spending, no extra fees and a rate that stays the same. Compare that estimate with the next statement to see how the balance actually moved.

If you have several cards, compare where the extra payment goes

Start by covering every card’s minimum. With the money left in your card repayment budget, you can model putting the extra towards the highest-interest card first (avalanche) or the smallest balance first (snowball). Use the same monthly budget for both comparisons.

See the worked avalanche and snowball example to compare the first card cleared, the final payoff month and the interest paid.

When a 0% balance transfer is worth comparing

Compare the transfer fee plus any interest you could pay after the offer expires with the interest you’d expect on the current card over the same repayment plan. For example, a 3% fee on £2,000 costs £60 upfront. Check how much of the balance could still be outstanding when the offer ends, what rate would apply then, and what minimum payments the new card requires.

If the minimum payments are hard to cover

If you’re behind on essentials such as rent or council tax, or can’t cover your card minimums, you can find free, confidential debt advice through MoneyHelper. An adviser can help you look at the whole budget and work out which bills need attention first.
