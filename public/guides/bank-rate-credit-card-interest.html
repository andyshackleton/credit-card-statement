Credit card interest

Does the Bank of England base rate affect credit cards?

On 5 November 2026, the Bank of England announced that Bank Rate had risen from 3.75% to 4.00%. Some credit cards have standard rates that move with Bank Rate. If your provider has told you that your card’s rate changed, the difference in pounds depends on how much of your balance is charged at that rate.

About £0.21 more in interest per month

This estimates the change in interest, with the balance held steady for a month and no compounding. Your minimum payment is shown on your statement. The amount actually charged can vary with your balance, payment dates and card terms.

Check which rate applies to your balance

Check the notice from your own card provider for the new rate and the date it takes effect. A change in Bank Rate may pass through to a linked standard rate, subject to your card terms and any cap or floor. Use the actual change in the simple annual rate for the balance you’re checking. If different rates apply to purchases, transfers or cash withdrawals, calculate each affected portion separately.

What if some of the balance is on a promotional rate?

Check when the offer ends and what rate applies afterwards. Halifax, for example, says a Bank Rate change leaves its promotional balances unaffected while the offer is active. If £700 of a £1,000 balance is on an unchanged promotion and £300 is charged at the rate that rose, enter £300. A 0.25 percentage point rise would add about 6p a month in that example.

Where to find the figures

Your banking app or online account can show your current balance. Check your latest statement and the rate-change notice for the rate that applies, the interest charged and the payment due. If the statement gives only an effective rate or APR, check the notice or ask your provider for the simple rate change.
